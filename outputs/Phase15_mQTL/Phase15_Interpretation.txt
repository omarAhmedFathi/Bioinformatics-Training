Phase 15: SNP-Metabolite Association (mQTL) Mapping
====================================================

Dataset: synthetic_obesity_metabolome_data
  Samples: 156  |  SNPs: 67735  |  Metabolites: 32

METHODOLOGY
-----------
Unadjusted: Simple linear regression  metabolite ~ genotype  (no covariates).
Adjusted:   Linear mixed model (EMMAX approach):
  1. Eigendecompose GRM to decorrelate samples.
  2. Estimate variance-component ratio (delta = sigma_g/sigma_e) via
     profile REML under the null model for each metabolite.
  3. Weighted regression incorporating Sex + PC1-10 as fixed-effect
     covariates and the GRM as a random effect, controlling for
     population stratification and cryptic relatedness.

RESULTS
-------
Total tests per run: 2,167,520 (67,735 SNPs x 32 metabolites)
Bonferroni threshold: p < 2.31e-08

Genomic inflation factor (lambda):
  Unadjusted: 1.0181
  Adjusted:   0.9973

Genome-wide significant hits: Unadjusted = 27, Adjusted = 15
Suggestive hits (p < 1e-5):   Unadjusted = 429, Adjusted = 285
Overlap at suggestive level:  189 shared, 240 unadjusted-only, 96 adjusted-only

INTERPRETATION
--------------
Comparing adjusted versus unadjusted scans demonstrates the effect of controlling
for population structure and relatedness.  A lambda closer to 1.0 in the adjusted
scan confirms that the mixed model effectively accounts for confounding.

With only 156 samples, statistical power is limited; large-scale mQTL studies
(QMDiab, TwinsUK, KORA) typically require 500+ samples for robust discovery.
Suggestive hits here are candidates for replication.

BIOLOGICAL CONTEXT
------------------
BCAA mQTLs (valine, leucine, isoleucine):  commonly map to BCAT1/2, BCKDHA/B.
Acylcarnitine mQTLs (C3, C5, C6, C16):    linked to CPT1A, ACADM, ACADL.
Bile acid mQTLs (taurocholate, GCDC):      associated with SLCO1B1, UGT enzymes.
Xanthine mQTLs (caffeine, paraxanthine):   strongly driven by CYP1A2 variation.
Purine mQTLs (hypoxanthine, xanthine):     linked to XDH (xanthine dehydrogenase).
GPC/lipid mQTLs:                           map to LPCAT, PLA2 family genes.
